AP RERA Compliance: Critical Jurisprudence on Project Registration & Buyer Remedies

Published: October 2026 • Author: Telugu Law House • Reading Time: 7 Minutes

Sample Legal Article Notice

This article is a sample academic commentary prepared for demonstration and informational purposes. The interpretations of statutes and decisions are intended for scholarly review and should not be relied upon as legal advice for any real-world real estate transaction.

1. Statutory Context & Scope of AP RERA

The enactment of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016 (RERA) fundamentally reshaped the legal relationship between property developers (promoters) and allottees (homebuyers) across India. In the State of Andhra Pradesh, the Andhra Pradesh Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Rules, 2017 established the institutional framework for AP RERA, headquartered at Amaravati.

Prior to RERA, allottees were largely relegated to prolonged civil suits for specific performance under the Specific Relief Act 1963, or summary consumer forums under the Consumer Protection Act. The creation of a dedicated regulatory authority with powers to issue binding directions, impose interim injunctions, and order refunds with statutory interest marked an unprecedented shift toward transparency.

2. Mandatory Project Registration & Escrow Mandates

Section 3 of the Act establishes that no promoter shall advertise, market, book, sell, or offer for sale any plot, apartment, or building in any real estate project exceeding 500 square meters or eight apartments without prior registration with the Authority.

Crucially, Section 4(2)(l)(D) mandates that 70% of the amounts realized for the real estate project from the allottees must be deposited in a separate dedicated bank account maintained in a scheduled bank to cover the cost of construction and the land cost, and can be withdrawn only in proportion to the percentage of completion of the project, certified by an engineer, architect, and chartered accountant.

“The statutory requirement under Section 18 of RERA is unconditional and absolute: where a promoter fails to deliver possession within the agreed timeline, the allottee's right to refund with statutory interest cannot be defeated by contractual force majeure clauses that lack external causality.”

3. Delay in Possession: Section 18 Remedies

Section 18 of the Act provides the principal statutory remedy for allottees when a developer fails to complete or give possession of an apartment, plot, or building in accordance with the terms of the agreement for sale:

  • Right of Exit with Refund: If the allottee wishes to withdraw from the project, the promoter is liable to return the entire amount received along with interest at the prescribed rate (State Bank of India Highest Marginal Cost of Lending Rate + 2%), in addition to statutory compensation.
  • Right of Continuation with Monthly Delay Interest: Where the allottee chooses not to withdraw from the project, the promoter must pay interest for every month of delay until the actual physical handing over of possession with occupancy certification.

4. Five-Year Structural Defect Liability (Section 14)

Under Section 14(3) of RERA, in case any structural defect or any other defect in workmanship, quality, or provision of services is brought to the notice of the promoter within a period of five years from the date of handing over possession, it shall be the duty of the promoter to rectify such defects without further charge within thirty days. In the event of failure, the aggrieved allottee is entitled to receive appropriate compensation.

5. Key Judicial Precedents of the High Court of Andhra Pradesh

Recent judicial review decisions rendered by the High Court of Andhra Pradesh under Article 226 of the Constitution have reaffirmed key procedural safeguards:

In a series of landmark writ petitions, the High Court held that orders of refund and recovery certificates issued by AP RERA under Section 40 of the Act are enforceable as arrears of land revenue through the District Collector, and execution cannot be stymied by ongoing insolvency petitions where the project itself has not been admitted to CIRP prior to the order.

6. Conclusion & Takeaways for Litigants

For buyers and developers operating in Amaravati, Vijayawada, and Visakhapatnam, adherence to AP RERA compliance timelines is non-negotiable. Agreements for sale must strictly mirror the Model Form prescribed under the AP Rules, avoiding one-sided forfeiture clauses that have been repeatedly invalidated by the Supreme Court of India in Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure Ltd. v. Govindan Raghavan.

Intellectual Property Rights

Protecting enterprise innovations, brands, and proprietary creations through trademark prosecution, copyright advisory, and commercial court enforcement.

IP & Commercial Practice

Safeguarding Intangible Capital Across Andhra Pradesh

In an innovation-driven economy, intellectual property constitutes one of an organization’s most valuable balance sheet assets. From the biotechnology and pharmaceutical corridors of Visakhapatnam to agricultural producers and technology enterprises across Andhra Pradesh, our IP practice provides end-to-end guidance from clearance searches to High Court infringement actions.

We represent clients before the Trade Marks Registry, Patent Office, Copyright Board, and Commercial Courts constituted under the Commercial Courts Act 2015.

Types of Matters Handled

Trademark Filings & Oppositions

Comprehensive brand clearance, trademark filing, responding to examination reports, and conducting opposition hearings.

Commercial Infringement Suits

Actions for trademark infringement, passing-off, copyright piracy, and securing interim injunctions before Commercial Courts.

IP Licensing & Technology Transfers

Structuring technology licensing agreements, brand franchise deeds, and non-disclosure and intellectual property assignment contracts.

Geographical Indications & Agri-IP

Advisory on Geographical Indications (GI) of Andhra Pradesh, agricultural patents, and plant variety protection under the PPV&FR Act.

Chambers IP Enforcement Approach

  • Ex-Parte Anton Piller Relief: Moving Commercial Courts for appointment of local commissioners to raid counterfeit hubs, seize infringing goods, and secure records.
  • Pre-Action Cease & Desist Notices: Issuing authoritative statutory notices to prevent escalation while firmly placing infringers on constructive notice.
  • Commercial Court Pre-Institution Mediation: Navigating mandatory mediation under Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act unless urgent interim relief is claimed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Infringement is a statutory remedy available strictly to the proprietor of a registered trademark under the Trade Marks Act 1999 upon proving deceptive similarity. Passing-off is a common law tort that protects the established goodwill and reputation of an unregistered brand against misleading representation by competitors.
The Commercial Courts Act establishes specialized Commercial Divisions with strict procedural timelines: mandatory discovery and disclosure of all documents with pleadings, strict 120-day forfeiture on filing written statements, case management hearings, and time-bound summary judgments.
A registered trademark in India is valid for a period of 10 years from the date of application. It can be renewed indefinitely every 10 years upon payment of the prescribed statutory renewal fees.

Labour & Employment Law

Strategic employment advisory, workplace compliance, POSH framework guidance, and representation before Industrial Tribunals across Andhra Pradesh.

Workplace & Industrial Advisory

Balancing Institutional Governance and Statutory Rights

Andhra Pradesh’s manufacturing corridors in Sri City, Visakhapatnam, and Vijayawada, alongside burgeoning service sectors, operate under rigorous state and central labour legislation. Our employment law group advises corporations, academic institutions, and senior management on structuring workplace contracts, conducting disciplinary domestic inquiries, and navigating industrial disputes.

We represent clients before Labour Courts, Industrial Tribunals, the Commissioner for Workmen’s Compensation, and in writ proceedings before the High Court of Andhra Pradesh at Amaravati.

Types of Matters Handled

Industrial Disputes & Strikes

Representation in collective bargaining, conciliation before Deputy Labour Commissioners, and references before Industrial Tribunals.

POSH Compliance & Inquiries

Internal Committee (IC) constitution, compliance with the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013, and independent inquiry advisory.

Executive Contracts & Non-Competes

Drafting senior leadership employment agreements, confidentiality covenants, severance terms, and non-solicitation clauses.

Domestic Inquiries & Termination

Framing charge-sheets, appointment of inquiry officers, adhering to principles of natural justice, and defending wrongful termination claims.

Our Approach to Labour Matters

  • Preventative Policy Structuring: Developing legally compliant employee handbooks, code of conduct charters, and anti-harassment policies adapted to regional laws.
  • Meticulous Inquiry Protocols: Ensuring domestic inquiries strictly satisfy natural justice requirements to withstand scrutiny in subsequent writ or tribunal appeals.
  • Transition to Central Labour Codes: Guiding enterprises through wage restructuring and compliance adjustments in light of the four new Central Labour Codes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act 1872, any agreement that restrains anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business is void to that extent. Post-termination non-compete covenants are generally unenforceable in Indian courts, whereas reasonable confidentiality and non-solicitation covenants can be enforced.
Every workplace with 10 or more employees must constitute an IC comprising: (1) A Presiding Officer who is a senior woman employee, (2) At least two members committed to the cause of women or possessing legal experience, and (3) One external member from an NGO or familiar with sexual harassment issues. At least one-half of the members must be women.
Under Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act 1947, a workman who has been in continuous service for not less than one year cannot be retrenched until: (1) Given one month’s notice in writing or wages in lieu thereof, (2) Paid compensation equivalent to 15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service, and (3) Notice is served upon the appropriate Government authority.

Property Law & Real Estate (AP RERA)

Comprehensive title diligence, revenue record verification, AP RERA buyer disputes, partition suits, and commercial lease structuring across Andhra Pradesh.

Real Estate Practice

Deep Understanding of Andhra Pradesh Land Jurisprudence

Property transactions and land disputes in Andhra Pradesh involve a layered intersection of civil enactments and complex state revenue records. From verifying 30-year link documents across Sub-Registrar offices to scrutinizing Webland entries (Pahani, Adangal, Form 1B, and Pattadar Passbooks), our chambers delivers reliable title opinions and robust litigation representation.

In urban sectors—including Amaravati Capital Region (CRDA), Vijayawada, Guntur, and Visakhapatnam (VMRDA)—we advise on developer compliance, joint development agreements (JDA), and represent homebuyers and resident welfare associations before the AP Real Estate Regulatory Authority (AP RERA).

Types of Matters Handled

30-Year Title Search & Due Diligence

Exhaustive investigation of registered sale deeds, ancestral partition instruments, Encumbrance Certificates, and revenue entries.

AP RERA Litigation & Refunds

Complaints before AP RERA under Sections 18 & 31 for possession delay, interest compensation, structural defects, and statutory disclosures.

Partition Suits & Revenue Inquiries

Filing and defending partition suits among legal heirs, preliminary and final decrees, and challenges to mutation entries before RDOs & Joint Collectors.

NALA Conversion & Land Pooling

Legal scrutiny of Non-Agricultural Land Assessment (NALA) conversions, layout regularizations, and Land Pooling Scheme (LPS) allotments.

Our Property Diligence Process

  • Dual Verification: We cross-verify registered instruments from the Registration & Stamps Department with electronic village revenue records on the AP Webland portal.
  • Encumbrance & Litigational Tracing: Scrutinizing court registries for pending lis pendens suits under Section 52 of the Transfer of Property Act to prevent disputed purchases.
  • Water-tight Conveyancing: Drafting unambiguous sale deeds, gift settlements, release deeds, and Joint Development Agreements protecting client interests.

Frequently Asked Questions

A comprehensive title search typically requires: (1) Registered Link Documents covering at least 30 years, (2) Encumbrance Certificate (EC) for the uninterrupted period, (3) Latest Adangal/Pahani and 1B Namoona extracts from Webland, (4) Title Deed / Pattadar Passbook, (5) Approved building layout / CRDA / DTCP plan approvals, and (6) Property tax and revenue cess receipts.
Under Section 18 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016, if the promoter fails to complete or give possession of an apartment in accordance with the terms of the agreement, the allottee has the right to: (1) Withdraw from the project and demand full refund of the amount paid along with interest at the prescribed rate, or (2) Remain in the project and receive monthly delay compensation interest until actual delivery of possession.
As consistently held by the Supreme Court of India and the High Court of Andhra Pradesh, mutation entries in revenue records (such as Pahani or Pattadar Passbooks) are maintained primarily for fiscal and tax collection purposes and do not, by themselves, extinguish or create substantive legal title, which must derive from valid registered instruments or succession.
